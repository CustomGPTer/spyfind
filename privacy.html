Privacy policy

Last updated 23 November 2026. This policy covers the SpyFind app for Android (package uk.jj.spyfind) and this website. SpyFind is made by Jonathan Jackson, an individual developer in the United Kingdom, who can be reached by opening an issue on the project's GitHub page.

The short version

  • Everything SpyFind finds, and everything you save in it, is kept on your phone. It is never uploaded to the developer or to anyone else.
  • There are no accounts, no adverts, no analytics and no tracking.
  • The only thing fetched from the internet is the updated stalkerware list, from this site.
  • Tracker Watch's notes of where you were are deleted after 24 hours.

What stays on your phone

SpyFind keeps these in the app's private storage, which other apps cannot read:

  • Your scans and sweeps: the devices found, why each was flagged, and when.
  • Tracker Watch sightings: which tracker, when, and roughly where. These are deleted after 24 hours, or sooner from Settings.
  • Camera and mic alerts: which app used the camera or microphone, and when.
  • Room Sweep checklists, trusted devices, your settings and your app lock PIN, which is stored only as a salted hash.
  • PDF reports, which are made on the phone and only go where you share them.

Android's cloud backup and device transfer are switched off for SpyFind, so none of this is copied to a Google account.

Scanning

SpyFind listens for Bluetooth signals around you and asks the devices on your own Wi-Fi what they are. It never connects to, logs in to, blocks or changes anyone's device, and what it hears is not sent anywhere.

The spyware scan

The scan reads the list of apps on your phone and the access each one has, and compares them with the stalkerware list built into SpyFind. All of this happens on the phone. When you update the list, SpyFind downloads a file from this site; it sends nothing about your phone in doing so. Like any website, GitHub Pages sees your internet address when the file is fetched.
